# Selected Independent Research Impact

This page highlights selected examples where my published research was independently cited, reviewed, or extended by international researchers. It focuses on downstream research influence rather than citation totals alone.

## MuleTrack: A Lightweight Temporal Learning Framework for Money Mule Detection in Digital Payments

The paper received early independent recognition through its citation in *[Detection, Attribution, Narration: An End-to-End Pipeline for Explainable Money Mule Identification](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17586)*, presented at the **KDD 2026 Workshop on Machine Learning in Finance**. The citing work explicitly discusses *MuleTrack* as a Markov-chain framework for detecting money mule accounts in India’s UPI ecosystem and positions it alongside its own institution-level explainable AI pipeline based on **LightGBM, TreeSHAP and Large Language Models (LLMs)**. The citing study reports production deployment metrics of **89% confirmed-mule yield** and **60% incremental adverse detection**, demonstrating that *MuleTrack* has contributed to subsequent research on operational, explainable anti-money-laundering systems in the banking domain.

## Demand and Capacity Modelling in Healthcare Using Discrete Event Simulation

The paper received strong independent validation through its inclusion in a comprehensive review of **231 healthcare simulation studies**. The review was authored by researchers from **Tecnológico de Monterrey, Mexico**, and **Macquarie University, Australia**, and included Professor **Leopoldo Eduardo Cárdenas-Barrón**, an established operations-research scholar with more than **11,000 Scopus citations** and an **h-index of approximately 60**. The work was also referenced in the *European Journal of Operational Research* and Springer’s *Archives of Computational Methods in Engineering*, demonstrating international influence in healthcare capacity planning, patient-flow modelling and operational decision-making.

## Comparative Analysis of Transformer-Based Pre-Trained NLP Models

The research compared **BERT, RoBERTa and ALBERT** for multiclass sentiment classification, examining predictive performance and computational efficiency. Its findings were subsequently used by a **multi-university research team in Morocco**, which extended the comparison to CNN and transformer models using **Apache Spark NLP** for large-scale social-media analysis. This follow-on work demonstrates downstream influence in transformer benchmarking, distributed NLP and scalable social-media intelligence.

## Multi-Class Sentiment Classification Using Machine Learning and Deep Learning Techniques

The paper received independent international validation when it was cited and extended five years later by a **multi-university research team in Thailand** specialising in machine learning, natural-language processing and text mining. The subsequent study expanded the original research into cross-domain sentiment classification using **BERT, CNN, LSTM** and established machine-learning models, demonstrating sustained methodological relevance in fine-grained sentiment analysis and domain generalisation.
